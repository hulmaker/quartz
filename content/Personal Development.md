Discipline is tightly bound with other factors. It's impossible to reach your full potential and give the perfect performance without having every mental aspect in place. And if you have most of them in place, it's robust and the structure hold it's own weight. See [Mental factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_factors_(Buddhism))

A [[Discipline]] is only a condition to achieve [[Consistency]]. To do that you need a competing interest. Something that is worth the pain and suffering. You need to discover what you really want.


**Types of desires**:
- **Internal**: things that give you energy when you imagine them, even if they’re hard.
- **Social**: things you’ve absorbed from family, culture, or peers.
- **Avoidance**: things that come from fear or insecurity. (Want to be rich not to be failure.)

Tests:
- **Energy test**: When you imagine living that reality, do you feel _a spark of energy_— or do you feel _tension, or a sense of “should”_?
- **Suffering test**: If you had to suffer for it for a year, would you _still_ want it?
- **Childhood test**: Is there a version of this desire that existed in you as a kid, before the world told you what success looks like?
- **Solitude test**: If nobody ever knew you achieved it — if no one could admire or validate you — would it _still matter to you_?
