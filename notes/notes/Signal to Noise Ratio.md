The signal-to-noise ratio is a **priority management framework**: by choose **"signal"** and eliminate/delegate the **"noise"**. 

> Having high signal (and low noise) is what differentiates those who produce big outcomes from those who drift.
> \- Steve Jobs: 

## Key Points
- “Signal vs Noise”: distinguishing the **critical tasks (“signal”)** from everything else that distracts or dilutes your energy **(“noise”)**.
- **80 % signal : 20 % noise** — most of your energy goes to the few things that matter, and only a small portion to everything else.
- Steve Jobs is cited as someone who mastered this: focusing only on the top few priorities, and treating everything else as noise. - Practically: pick 3-5 things that are mission-critical for today/this week; everything else (unless it directly supports those) is noise and should be delegated, postponed or removed. (From your transcript: divide tasks into a “signal” column and “noise” column.)
- Life/time is finite (“zero-sum game”): time spent on noise subtracts from time available for signal. (Transcript)
- Saying _no_ to good ideas is necessary: focus isn’t just about saying yes to the right thing, but saying no to many good but non-essential things. ## Takeaways – actionable

- Each morning (or week) create two lists/columns:
    - **Signal**: tasks that align with your key goal(s) and must be done.
    - **Noise**: tasks that distract, lesser priority, or can be delegated.
- Limit your “signal” tasks to a small number (3-5) so you can give them full focus.
- When a new task/idea arrives, ask: _Does this move my mission forward?_ If not, classify as noise.
- Design your environment/processes to reduce noise: interruptions, reactive tasks, trivial commitments.
- Recognise that many “good” tasks are still noise relative to your bigger goal.
- Commit to saying no: if you wake up thinking about a fantastic new idea but you have signal tasks, that idea may have to wait.
- Use feedback loops: look at how much of your time was signal vs noise; iterate to shift more time to signal.

## Criteria: How to decide “signal” vs “noise”
**Signal** = tasks or activities that satisfy all or most of:
- Directly advance your core mission or top-level goal.
- Produce high leverage / high impact for you or your organisation.
- Require your unique contribution (you are the person to do it).
- If left undone, would cause major delay or risk to your main objective.
- Scheduled/planned and aligned with your priority list.
**Noise** = tasks or activities that:
- Do not clearly link to the mission or key goal.
- Are low-impact or low-leverage (nice-to-have).
- Can be delegated, automated or eliminated.
- Are reactive rather than proactive (emails, unexpected meetings, trivial tasks).
- Distract you from your signal tasks.
