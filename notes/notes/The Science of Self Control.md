## 🧠 Core Ideas
- **Self-control** == **ability to monitor internal conflict**
- Your ability to monitor internal conflict is **drained by stress and emotional regulation**.
- The **Anterior Mid-Cingulate Cortex (AMCC)** is responsible for monitoring internal conflict. [[Willpower & Anterior Mid-Cingulate Cortex]]

## ⚠️ Common Misconception
> We think self-control = emotional regulation.  
> **But it's not!** They actually oppose each other.

## 😣 What Drains the Internal Conflict Monitor (Self-Control)
- **Emotional regulation**: does not control emotions. It only suppresses internal awareness. Which weakens the ability to monitor internal conflict, thus weakens self-control.
- **[[Stress]]**: externalizes attention to problems - at work, tiger in nature, police officer... This **disconnects us from internal awareness**, disrupting self-control.

> [!tip]
> If you stop monitoring, you lose self-control — **not the other way around**.


## 🛠️ Practice: Re-internalize Attention
- Take **10–15 minutes** when stressed to check in:
    - _"How am I feeling right now?"_
- This builds awareness and restores control through [[Meditation]]
- Kind of related to [[Moc přítomného okamžiku]]

## Notes
- You don’t overcome addiction by willpower alone.
- You overcome it by **paying attention to internal conflict** — consistently.
- Athletes, survivors, and elders often have strong self-control.
- Why? They're **in touch with their internal states** — they monitor themselves well.
- Can help with [[Motivational crisis]] and [[Discipline]] and also [[Consistency]]
